About the Pink and White Terraces

The Pink and White Terraces were a natural wonder located near Lake Rotomahana in New Zealand's North Island. They were famous for their stunning silica formations created by mineral-rich geothermal waters flowing over the landscape for thousands of years.

During the 1800s, visitors from around the world traveled to see these spectacular formations. Many people referred to them as the "Eighth Wonder of the World".

The terraces were formed by hot springs depositing layers of silica, creating giant stair-like pools filled with warm water.

The White Terraces

The White Terraces (Te Tarata) were the larger of the two terrace systems. They stretched down a hillside in a series of gleaming white pools and steps.

Visitors could walk across many of the terraces and even bathe in some of the warm pools. The White Terraces were considered one of the most beautiful geothermal features on Earth.

The Pink Terraces

The Pink Terraces (Otukapuarangi) were known for their delicate pink coloring. The soft pink shades came from trace minerals contained within the geothermal waters.

Many visitors considered the Pink Terraces even more beautiful than the White Terraces because of their unique color and elegant shape.

Historical Timeline

Before European Arrival

Māori communities knew the terraces well and regarded them as an important natural feature of the region.

1840s–1880s

Tourists from around the world visited the terraces, making them one of New Zealand's most famous attractions.

1886

Mount Tarawera erupted violently, dramatically changing the landscape around Lake Rotomahana.

Modern Day

Scientists continue researching whether parts of the terraces may still exist beneath sediments or underwater.

The Mount Tarawera Eruption

On 10 June 1886, Mount Tarawera erupted in one of New Zealand's most powerful volcanic events. The eruption devastated nearby settlements and dramatically altered the surrounding environment.

For many years it was believed that the Pink and White Terraces had been completely destroyed during the eruption.

The eruption lasted for several hours and created a massive rift across the landscape stretching for many kilometers.

Legacy and Scientific Research

The Pink and White Terraces remain one of New Zealand's greatest lost natural wonders. Historical paintings, photographs, maps, and written accounts provide valuable information about their appearance.

Modern researchers have used historical surveys, sonar mapping, and geological studies to investigate whether remnants of the terraces may still survive beneath Lake Rotomahana.

Although the original terraces are no longer visible, they remain an important part of New Zealand's geological and cultural history.

Interesting Facts

  • The terraces were created naturally by geothermal activity.
  • They were considered one of the world's greatest tourist attractions in the 1800s.
  • Lake Rotomahana changed dramatically after the 1886 eruption.
  • The Pink Terraces were famous for their rare pink color.
  • The White Terraces were larger than the Pink Terraces.
  • Scientists still debate whether parts of the terraces survive underground.
