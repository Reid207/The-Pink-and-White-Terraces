The Eighth Wonder

The Pink and White Terraces were silica formations created by geothermal waters over thousands of years. This caused the waters to become a hot spring because of the aching heat. Which then formed the natural bubbles we see today.

It was known around the world as the “Eighth Wonder of the World”.

Historical Timeline

Before European Arrival

The Māori valued the Terraces culturally and they would regularly bathe and clean in the hot springs. Many Maori lived full lives there and there were many cultural villages. They would also bathe in the hot springs to heal wounds and fallen Pakeha soilders because of the amazing healing propertys in the hot spring waters.

1840s–1880s

International tourism grew rapidly which attracted a lot of Pakehas to our land of New Zealand. Which was great and amazing because others could learn the way of the Maori well before the colonisers of course.

1886 Eruption

Mount Tarawera erupted VIOLENTLY which reshaped the landscape destroying the Terraces and old Maori villages who lived there. Soon the the remants were filled with large amounts of water forming what we now know as Lake Rotomahana.

Modern Research

Now a days the Scientists continue investigating submerged remains of the Pink and White Terraces, they did this by using submarines and under water mapping technology for futher research of the Pink and White Terraces.

The Eruption

On 10 June 1886 Mount Tarawera erupted and destroyed the Terraces leaving lava, magma and remants are the hot springs. During the eruption it also destroyed many Maori homes and villages. It destroyed over 10,000 Kilometers of land.

Legacy

Today the Pink and White Terraces remain one of New Zealand’s most fascinating geological stories. Also it was said that there was once a 'Phantom Canoe' (Te Waka Rerenga Wairua) that travled past Mount Tarawera ten days before it erupted. It went missing only a second later once it went into the mist..
